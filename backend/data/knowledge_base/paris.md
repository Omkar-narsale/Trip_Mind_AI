# Paris Travel Intelligence & European City Guide

## Safety & Emergency Protocols
- **Emergency Numbers**: Dial 112 (European emergency unified number) or 17 (French National Police / Gendarmerie) and 15 (SAMU Medical Emergency).
- **Pickpocket Warning Zones**: Be vigilant on Paris Métro Line 1, Line 4, around Gare du Nord, Champ de Mars (Eiffel Tower gardens), Sacré-Cœur steps in Montmartre, and outside the Louvre pyramid. Keep bags zipped and worn in front in crowded train cars.
- **Classic Street Scams**: Ignore street petition clipboard groups, the "found gold ring" trick, and the "three-card monte" shell games around bridges over the Seine.

## Local Transit & Mobility Rules
- **RATP Métro & RER Network**: Paris has 16 Métro lines and 5 RER commuter express rail lines. Magnetic paper tickets (Ticket t+) have transitioned to digital Navigo Easy smartcards and contactless smartphone validation via Île-de-France Mobilités app.
- **Airport Transfers**: RER Line B connects Charles de Gaulle Airport (CDG) directly to central Paris (Châtelet-Les Halles / Gare du Nord) in approximately 35 minutes. Official taxis have fixed flat-rate fares (€56–€65) to central Right and Left Bank districts.

## Visa & Schengen Regulations
- **Schengen Visa Requirements**: Non-EU travelers must hold a valid Schengen Visa (or approved ETIAS travel authorization when applicable) and passport with at least 3 months validity beyond the intended departure date.
- **Travel Insurance**: Valid overseas travel health insurance with minimum medical coverage of €30,000 is legally mandated for Schengen entry.

## Cultural Etiquette & Dining Norms
- **The "Bonjour" Rule**: Always greet shopkeepers, café staff, and hotel concierges with a courteous *"Bonjour, Madame/Monsieur"* before asking a question or ordering. Omitting this is perceived as abrupt and rude.
- **Tipping & Service Charge**: Service is legally included in French café and restaurant bills by law (*service compris*, typically 15%). Leaving an additional €1 to €3 in small change is customary for attentive hospitality, but not compulsory.
