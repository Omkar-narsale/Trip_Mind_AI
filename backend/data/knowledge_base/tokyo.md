# Tokyo Travel Intelligence & Japan Urban Guide

## Safety & Emergency Protocols
- **Emergency Police**: Dial 110. Japan's neighborhood police boxes (*Koban*) are located at nearly every major intersection and train station to assist with directions, lost items, and reporting emergencies.
- **Fire & Ambulance**: Dial 119.
- **Earthquake Preparedness**: Tokyo buildings are constructed to stringent seismic codes. During seismic tremors, protect your head under sturdy furniture, stay away from glass windows, and wait until shaking subsides before evacuating.

## Local Transit & Mobility Rules
- **Suica & Pasmo IC Cards**: Contactless IC smartcards (digital Suica/Pasmo on Apple Wallet or Welcome Suica cards) are essential for tap-and-go travel across JR East lines, Tokyo Metro, Toei Subway, and convenience store payments.
- **Train Etiquette**: Keep smartphones on silent mode (*manner mode*) aboard subway trains. Refrain from speaking on voice phone calls. Maintain quiet voices and queue precisely on marked platform lines before boarding.
- **Last Train Curfew**: Tokyo's subway and rail lines do not run 24 hours. The last trains typically depart between midnight (00:00) and 00:45 AM. After this hour, travelers must rely on taxis or wait until 05:00 AM.

## Visa & Entry Regulations
- **Visit Japan Web**: Travelers should pre-register customs and immigration declarations online via the official Visit Japan Web portal to generate fast-track QR codes prior to landing at Narita (NRT) or Haneda (HND).
- **Tourist Visa Exemption**: Over 70 nationalities enjoy visa exemptions for short tourism stays up to 90 days. Other passport holders must obtain a single-entry or double-entry eVisa.

## Cultural Etiquette & Local Laws
- **Trash Disposal**: Public trash cans are rare on Tokyo streets (removed following historical security protocols). Always carry a small bag to carry personal waste back to your hotel or dispose of bottles in recycling bins beside vending machines.
- **Tipping Norm**: Tipping is NOT practiced in Japan and can cause confusion or embarrassment. Exceptional service is viewed as the baseline standard included in the bill.
