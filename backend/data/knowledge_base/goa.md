# Goa Travel Intelligence & Safety Guide

## Safety & Emergency Protocols
- **Emergency Police**: Dial 112 (Central Police Control Room) or 100.
- **Medical Emergency**: Dial 108 for emergency ambulance services in Goa.
- **Beach Safety & Lifeguards**: Drishti Marine manages certified lifeguards along active beaches (Calangute, Baga, Candolim, Colva, Palolem). Swim only between the designated red-and-yellow flags. Never swim in the sea after sunset or under red flags (monsoon / rough sea hazard).
- **Public Drinking Ban**: Drinking alcohol on public beaches and roads is strictly prohibited by Goa Police under the Tourist Places Protection Act, carrying spot fines up to ₹2,000 or ₹10,000 for groups. Drink only within licensed beach shacks and bars.

## Local Transit & Mobility Rules
- **Motorcycle Taxis (Pilots)**: Goa is famous for licensed motorcycle taxis locally called "Pilots" (bearing yellow registration plates). They are ideal for quick, solo short-distance trips between beaches. Agree on the fare before boarding.
- **Car & Scooter Rentals**: Self-drive two-wheelers and hatchbacks are ubiquitous. Always wear a BIS-certified helmet; Goa traffic police strictly fine unhelmeted riders. Always carry an original driving license and rental agreement.
- **Taxi Fares & GoaMiles**: Traditional roadside tourist taxis do not use digital meters; agree on the fixed fare upfront or use the government-backed GoaMiles app to avoid overcharging. Pre-paid taxi counters operate at Dabolim Airport (GOI) and Manohar International Airport Mopa (GOX).

## Visa, Entry & Identification
- **Domestic Travelers**: Require a valid government photo ID (Aadhaar, Voter ID, Passport, or Driving License) for hotel check-ins and airport security.
- **International Visitors**: Must possess a valid Indian Visa or approved Indian e-Tourist Visa (30-day, 1-year, or 5-year duration) along with a passport valid for at least 6 months with 2 blank pages.

## Cultural Etiquette & Local Laws
- **Beachwear Restraint**: Swimsuits and bikinis are standard on the sand and within private resort pools, but avoid walking through village markets, churches, and urban streets in swimwear. Wear modest clothing covering shoulders and knees when visiting Old Goa churches (Basilica of Bom Jesus) or temples (Mangueshi Temple).
- **Water Sports Verification**: Engage only with government-registered water sports operators offering life jackets and safety briefing before jet skiing, parasailing, or banana rides.
